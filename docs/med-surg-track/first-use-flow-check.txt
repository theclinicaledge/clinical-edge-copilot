Clinical Edge Rewind: first-use flow check
Updated October 18, 2026 Pacific

Purpose
Observe whether a new learner can understand and navigate Rewind without coaching. This is a usability check, not clinical validation. Use the private preview and fictional scenarios only.

Starting point
Use a separate test browser profile with a completed assignment. Do not clear or replace the founder's saved responses. Ask the learner to think aloud. Do not point out buttons or explain Rewind before they encounter its instructions.

Tasks and success criteria
1. From the completed shift, find optional practice. Learner finds Explore Rewind and recognizes that the next assignment is also available.
2. Explain what Rewind does in their own words. Learner understands that this version's facts govern the response, the action may stay the same, and practice preserves original answers.
3. Read a situation and choose a response. Learner finds patient details again when needed and understands what an incomplete selection requires.
4. Check the response. Learner can distinguish their chosen action from its feedback and identify the findings supporting the explanation.
5. Stop after one situation. Learner reaches the partial summary or returns to the debrief without believing all three are mandatory.
6. Reopen and continue. Learner finds saved progress and reaches an untried situation.
7. Review completed feedback. Learner opens the saved response without submitting a new attempt and can choose an explicit retry if desired.
8. Move on. Learner reaches the next assignment or the track after A3, without being stranded in the modal.

Record for each task
Completed unaided / needed a hint / could not complete; exact confusing wording or action; device/browser; observed navigation. Distinguish observed behavior from the learner's opinion. Do not record patient information.

Fix criteria
Any blocked path, lost response, wrong feedback, or accidental overwrite blocks release. If a learner needs navigation coaching, revise that point and repeat that task with someone unfamiliar with the feature. Do not count a coached repeat as first-use success.
